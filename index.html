📊 How the 0–10 rating is calculated real CAASPP

The 0–10 rating and the decade history are REAL, computed from California's official statewide tests (CAASPP Smarter Balanced) — the same test‑score signal GreatSchools uses — turned into a 1–10 statewide rank:

  1. Proficiency % = share of students who Met or Exceeded the standard, using ELA + Math (all grades, all students). ELA and Math are combined weighted by the number tested: prof = (ELA%·ELAtested + Math%·Mathtested) / (ELAtested + Mathtested).
  2. Statewide decile (1–10) = rank this school's proficiency against every CA school that year: rating = ceil(percentile_rank × 10), clamped to 1–10. (So 10 = top ~10% in the state, 5 ≈ median.)
  3. Decade history & trend = the same decile computed for each available year (2015–2019, 2022–2024; 2020–21 had no testing); the line's least‑squares slope gives Improving / Stable / Declining.

Also REAL from these files: enrollment and the “% Met/Above (CAASPP)” figure in each detail panel.

Modeled (placeholder) fields — not from a data feed yet, shown for layout and clearly badged: Academic progress, Equity, College ready, Students:teacher ratio, Low‑income %, Graduation rate. These are deterministic estimates seeded by the school id; join CA DOE DataQuest (grad / A‑G / AP) and the CAASPP growth model to make them real.

Why it can differ from GreatSchools: GreatSchools' overall number is a weighted blend of several ratings — test scores plus academic growth, graduation / college‑readiness (A‑G, AP/IB, SAT/ACT) and an equity component — with grade‑level weights that are periodically re‑calibrated. This app currently uses the test‑score component only, so a school can land about one point above or below the GreatSchools composite (e.g. Vinci Park ≈ 60% proficient → our decile 9 vs GreatSchools 8).

School names, locations, districts and grade spans are REAL (CA Dept. of Education directory). Assignment is a district-aware nearest-school approximation of official attendance boundaries. Ratings & history are modeled deterministic estimates (badged in-app). Geocoding tries the US Census Geocoder, then falls back to a built-in ZIP/city centroid table so it works on locked networks.
